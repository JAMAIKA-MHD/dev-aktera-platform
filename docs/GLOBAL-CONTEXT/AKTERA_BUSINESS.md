# AKTERA — Business & Commercial Context

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** Working business context

---

## 1. Business identity

AKTERA is building a B2B Customer Engagement Platform for businesses, initially focused on interactive/gamified marketing campaigns.

The broader company ambition is marketing/customer-experience infrastructure, not a permanent focus on gamification alone.

---

## 2. Core problem

The project materials describe several recurring frictions for businesses running marketing campaigns:

- custom development can be expensive;
- agency-led campaign development can create delays;
- campaign assets and logic may not be reusable;
- businesses may have limited autonomy to modify campaigns;
- customer data can remain dependent on external providers;
- measurement and optimization can be weak;
- generic social, SMS, form, and agency workflows do not provide a unified customer-engagement infrastructure.

AKTERA aims to give businesses a reusable platform for launching interactive experiences, collecting consented data, managing rewards, and measuring results.

---

## 3. Initial customer profile

The initial focus is B2C businesses that:

- interact with meaningful customer volumes;
- run campaigns or activations repeatedly;
- need engagement rather than only broadcast communication;
- benefit from collecting useful first-party/zero-party information;
- want to launch without building an entire custom campaign system.

Initial sectors discussed in the project materials include:

- agro-food and beverages;
- cosmetics and parapharmacy;
- retail / modern commerce;
- e-commerce;
- electronics and appliances;
- restaurants and franchises.

Broader sectors may be explored later.

---

## 4. Additional customer channels / segments

Potential later users include:

- digital agencies;
- communication/activation agencies;
- marketing consultants;
- franchise networks;
- multi-brand groups.

Agency use is strategically interesting because one agency relationship can potentially expose AKTERA to multiple brands.

---

## 5. Core business value proposition

For a business, AKTERA aims to provide:

- faster creation and deployment of interactive campaigns;
- less dependence on custom development for every campaign;
- reusable campaign infrastructure;
- ownership/access to collected data;
- controlled participant and reward flows;
- better fraud/integrity controls than informal social-media mechanics;
- real-time campaign measurement;
- local-market adaptation.

---

## 6. Local-market positioning

The competition materials emphasize local adaptation such as:

- Algerian payment ecosystem support;
- local hosting/data considerations;
- local reward types;
- OTP/contact verification;
- SMS/WhatsApp-related workflows;
- local operational and regulatory realities.

These should be treated as strategic product directions and capabilities to validate progressively, not assumed to be fully live today.

---

## 7. Current commercial strategy

The near-term commercial approach is centered on **demonstration and pilot validation**.

The intended motion is roughly:

1. build a credible demo-ready product;
2. demonstrate it to prospective brands;
3. secure initial pilot/reference campaigns;
4. learn from real usage;
5. improve the platform;
6. convert successful pilots into recurring relationships;
7. use references and agency relationships to expand.

A visible successful campaign can become evidence for future prospects.

---

## 8. ProtoMarket role

ProtoMarket is viewed as an accelerator for the transition from an advanced MVP toward a production-grade pilot capability.

The intended value is not simply adding features. It is enabling the project to strengthen:

- backend ownership;
- cloud/deployment capability;
- security;
- integrations;
- reliability;
- user experience;
- operational readiness;
- first real customer deployments.

---

## 9. Business assumptions versus validated facts

Early commercial numbers in previous project documents — including exact prices and TAM/SAM/SOM figures — should **not be treated as canonical validated facts**.

They are currently better classified as:

`HYPOTHESIS`

until stronger market research, customer interviews, pricing experiments, pipeline evidence, and real deployments validate them.

Do not use an old presentation number as if it were a confirmed market fact without checking its status.

---

## 10. Current commercial learning agenda

The team is still learning important parts of the market, including:

- which customer segment has the strongest urgency;
- which campaign use cases generate real willingness to pay;
- how customers perceive the value of the platform versus agencies/custom development;
- acceptable pricing and packaging;
- which integrations are truly required for early customers;
- how much no-code autonomy customers actually need;
- what agencies could contribute to distribution;
- which features are essential for a successful pilot.

These are active learning questions, not settled doctrine.

---

## 11. Competitive positioning

The current strategic framing distinguishes AKTERA from:

- social-media-only campaign mechanics;
- custom agency development;
- internal development for large companies;
- international platforms that may not fit local requirements.

The intended differentiation is the combination of reusable customer-engagement infrastructure and local operational adaptation.

Specific competitor claims should be independently revalidated before being reused in formal materials.

---

## 12. Geographic ambition

The initial market is Algeria.

The broader vision includes possible expansion into North Africa / MENA and potentially other markets when justified by product maturity and commercial evidence.

Expansion timing and exact markets are not fixed here.

---

## 13. Business context rule for AI

AI systems must distinguish:

- verified current business facts;
- team decisions;
- current commercial experiments;
- hypotheses;
- historical claims from competition documents;
- long-term strategic vision.

When helping with market or business decisions, avoid silently converting an assumption into a fact.
